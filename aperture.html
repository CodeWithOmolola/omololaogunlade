Intake CRM Prototype

Urban Pathways (NYC nonprofit)

Internal Operations Tool

Workflow Audit · Product Spec · Prototype · Compliance Review

Base44

Prototype · Approved to Pilot by Compliance

Intake at Urban Pathways ran on spreadsheets and the intake coordinator's memory. She carried the full picture of every case in her head, which meant there was no shared view of who was waiting, where each case stood, or what had already happened. Every follow-up depended on her remembering to send it. Aperture was built to give that process a structure: one system from first contact onward, designed around how intake actually happened, not how a vendor CRM assumes it does.

Before building anything, I went to the head of compliance, not the end user. That conversation confirmed there was no existing CRM or internal system to conflict with. It also secured approval to workshop the tool with the intake coordinator and integrate it if it worked. From there, I met with the coordinator and translated her workflow into five requirements: a backlog, stage tracking, a progress log, automated communications, and a feedback loop. I weighed off-the-shelf options like Airtable and Monday.com, then chose a custom build in Base44 so the system could bend to her process instead of the reverse.

Rollout then stalled on a client-side dependency. The coordinator agreed to share her existing spreadsheets to populate and test the system, and they never came through. That taught me the part of deployment a spec can't cover. Access to data is a commitment you secure before building, not after. Next time, that's the first agreement on the table.

Product Decisions

Intake

One Record Per Case

Every person entering intake gets a single record that carries their current stage and a running progress log. The history of a case lives in the system instead of across spreadsheets and one person's memory, and a backlog view shows who's waiting at a glance.

Compliance

Sanctioned Before Built:

The first conversation was with compliance confirmed that no competing system existed internally, and getting approval to pilot before writing a line of spec, meant the tool had a path to adoption from day one, not a hurdle at the end.

Handoff

Follow-Ups That Don't Depend on Memory

Automated communications handle routine follow-ups, so they go out whether or not the coordinator remembers them, and a feedback loop [confirm: feedback from whom, e.g. clients, staff, or the coordinator herself] keeps the process improving as cases move through.
